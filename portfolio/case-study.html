The problem was my own filing

I had about a hundred sites saved in Raindrop and I could not find any of them.

Not because the tool was bad. Because saving a link takes two seconds and filing it properly takes two minutes, so I never did the second part. Six months of that and I had a folder called "inspiration" with ninety items in it, each one a title and a favicon, and no way to answer the only question I ever actually asked: what was that site with the stat strip that scrolled sideways?

Every tool I tried had the same shape. They all assumed the person saving would also do the describing. Nobody does the describing.

So the premise is that the filing has to happen without me. The whole interaction is one paste.

The constraint that shaped everything

I gave myself a hard rule: this costs nothing to run, forever. No trials, no free tiers that expire, no card on file anywhere.

That one rule made most of the architectural decisions for me, and it made them better than a budget would have. Screenshots run on free CI minutes instead of a $17 a month screenshot API. The AI runs on a free tier that allows twenty requests a day per model, which turned out to be the most interesting design problem in the project. A limit that tight forces you to be honest with people about what is and is not happening.

The vocabulary had to be the one I already think in

Information architecture

The first version let the AI pick its own words and produced exactly what you would expect: minimal, minimalist and clean minimal all describing the same page. So the vocabulary became a closed, faceted list: what the site is for, what kind of page it is, what blocks are on it, and how it looks.

The block list took two tries. Version one had Hero, Footer, Accordion, CTA Band. Every site ever made has a hero and a footer, so a tag naming only the family matches everything and finds nothing. I had written "never tag it as just hero" into the instructions from the very beginning and it never once worked.

It took me far too long to see why. The instruction said one thing and the menu it was choosing from said another.

The instruction was arguing with the menu, and the menu won.

Every value is family then variety now, and the families are the ones from the design system I work in every day. The words that come back are the words I would have searched for.

Colours are measured, not parsed

Analysis

The obvious way to get a site's palette is to read its stylesheet. It does not work. A stylesheet declares thousands of rules the page never paints, and how often a colour appears in CSS says nothing about how much of the screen it covers.

So Kivli opens the page in a real browser and weights every colour by the area it actually covers. Backgrounds minus whatever their children paint over them. Borders as perimeter times width. Text as ink, roughly 14% of the em square per character, because otherwise one paragraph outweighs an entire hero panel. Photographs are skipped on purpose: a palette taken from pixels reports the model's jumper in the hero shot, not the interface.

Then the measurement broke on its own terms. One site's teal sat on a single newsletter button. By area it was invisible. By chroma it was the most deliberate colour on the page.

Area is the wrong question to ask about an accent.

A call to action is deliberately small and deliberately the most saturated thing on the page. So two colours can be readmitted from under the floor, but only when they are painted on something clickable and highly saturated. Both halves matter: without the first test it readmits every stray tint, without the second it readmits every dark grey button. That same site's dark grey search button is correctly still left out.

The panel was right and my intuition was wrong

Typography

Kivli reports typefaces by role: headline, body and UI, accent. On one site I was certain it had made a mistake. The company has a typeface named after itself, and the panel had filed that face as chrome rather than as the interface face.

It was right.

The face named after the company
1,119characters, almost all footer legal
The face actually carrying the page
3,259characters of body copy

A face's role comes from where it is set, and the footer counts. I keep this one in the notes because it is the clearest case of the measurement knowing more than I did, and it is worth remembering before "fixing" a result that looks surprising.

Two progress bars, not one percentage

Status design

A hundred imported bookmarks is two hundred AI requests against a tier that allows sixty a day. "How long until this is done" has no honest answer.

But it has two answers. Screenshots cost free CI minutes and finish in hours. Tags cost the scarce thing and finish over days. One bar averaging those is wrong about both.

All your screenshots are in. Tagging resumes at 12:05 AM.

Keeping them apart is what lets the panel say that instead of saying 61%. The import bar also does not pulse, unlike the capture bar it otherwise copies. The capture bar animates because it describes a minute you are watching. This one describes three days you are not, and a stripe sliding under a still fill would be claiming activity it cannot vouch for.

A unit that lies about what it measures

Detail

I set the reading measure on the written pages to 74ch, which reads in the source like a 74 character line.

A ch is the width of the digit zero, one of the widest glyphs in a proportional face.

What the source says
74characters, apparently
What it actually fitted
102characters of real prose

The measure is 34em now, about 75 characters, arrived at by setting a long paragraph and dividing its width by the width of 100 characters of actual text. Measured, not calculated. It is the small version of the lesson the whole project kept teaching me.

What I deliberately did not build

  • Drag and drop folders A folder here is a tag, so one item can sit in two of them and the AI can file it on save. Real folders force a choice the library does not need to make, and drag and drop buys only re-tagging, which is already one click.
  • Sharing a single resource A resource is a URL. Sharing one is sending that URL. A share page wrapping one link is a redirect with branding on it.
  • A flat tag input It is a nicer interaction and it loses the faceting, which is the most important structural decision in the product.

What I would change

The tag vocabulary needs a periodic merge pass and the app does not prompt for one, so two family-only tags from an early version are still live and still matching everything. The merge tool exists. The reminder to use it does not.
