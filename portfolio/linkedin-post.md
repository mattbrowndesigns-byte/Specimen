## Version A, the main one

I could not find anything I had saved.

Not because my bookmarking app was bad. Because saving a link takes two seconds
and filing it properly takes two minutes, so I never did the second part. Six
months of that and I had ninety items in a folder called "inspiration" and no
way to answer the only question I ever actually asked: what was that site with
the stat strip that scrolled sideways?

So I built the thing that does the filing.

Kivli takes a URL and photographs the page at desktop and mobile, measures the
colours it actually paints, identifies the typefaces from their letterforms,
and tags it against the block vocabulary I already design in. One paste. No
forms, no folders, no second step.

Three things it taught me:

A tag that names only the family matches everything and finds nothing. "Hero"
is useless. "Hero Carousel" is a search result.

A palette taken from pixels reports the photography, not the interface. You
have to measure what the page paints and skip every image.

Area is the wrong question to ask about an accent. A call to action is
deliberately small and deliberately the most saturated thing on the page, so
weighting purely by area throws away the one colour the designer actually chose.

It runs on $0 a month, permanently. That constraint made more of the design
decisions than most briefs I have been given, and it made them better.

It is live and read only, so you can click straight into it. Link in the first
comment.
